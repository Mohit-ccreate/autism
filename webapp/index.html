📚 Evidence-Based Autism Learning Hub

Verified Peer-Reviewed Sources

Short, accurate, plain-language guides on neurodiversity, clinical pathways, and accommodations, citing public health guidelines and peer-reviewed reviews.

1. What Is Autism Spectrum Disorder?

Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference characterized by unique ways of social communication, sensory processing, and focused interests. It is not an illness to cure, but a different neurological operating system requiring understanding and tailored accommodations.

Source: WHO ICD-11 6A02; Lord et al. (2020) Nature Reviews Disease Primers.

2. Myths vs. Evidence-Based Facts

Myth: Autistic people lack empathy. Fact: Autistic individuals often experience intense affective empathy, but may communicate it differently (the "Double Empathy Problem").
Myth: Vaccines cause autism. Fact: Decades of global studies involving millions of children definitively prove vaccines do not cause autism.

Source: Milton (2012) Disability & Society; CDC Vaccine Safety Studies.

3. How Clinical Assessments Work

A formal diagnostic evaluation is a multidisciplinary process led by developmental pediatricians, child psychiatrists, or clinical psychologists. It utilizes standardized observational assessments (ADOS-2), comprehensive developmental history interviews (ADI-R), and speech-language assessments.

Source: NICE Guidelines CG142 (Adults) & CG170 (Children).

4. Talking to a Child About Neurodiversity

Use neurodiversity-affirming language that normalizes brain differences. Focus on their authentic strengths (passion, honesty, problem-solving) alongside strategies for sensory challenges. Emphasize that being autistic is part of who they are, not something wrong with them.

Source: Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN) Family Guide.

5. School and Workplace Accommodations

Effective accommodations are often simple and cost-free: noise-cancelling headphones, advance agendas for meetings, written instructions, quiet sensory retreat spaces, and flexible working hours. In India, statutory accommodations are protected under the RPwD Act 2016.

Source: Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016; UK Equality Act 2010.

6. Screening vs. Clinical Diagnosis

A screener (like AQ-10 or Q-CHAT) is a brief filter to identify individuals who may benefit from specialist consultation. A diagnosis requires a clinician to evaluate developmental history, differential diagnoses, and real-world adaptive functioning.

Source: American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) Identification Guidelines.

💡 "What We Tried That Didn't Work" (Honest Scientific Negative Findings)

Full Transparency

In accordance with scientific integrity guidelines, we document experiments, architectures, and design approaches that proved ineffective or scientifically invalid.

1. 2D CNNs on Raw Connectivity Matrices

What we tried: Applying 2D Convolutional Neural Networks directly to $200 \times 200$ functional correlation matrices treated as grayscale images.
Why it failed: 2D CNN kernels assume spatial translational invariance (e.g. pixels adjacent in an image share local spatial relationships). Brain atlas regions are arbitrarily ordered; permuting ROI indices changes CNN convolution output arbitrarily while preserving the exact same connectome. Linear models and graph networks are fundamentally more principled.

2. Deep Transformers on Unharmonized Full Edges (19,900)

What we tried: Training deep multi-head attention models on all $19,900$ raw functional connectivity edges without feature selection or ComBat harmonization.
Why it failed: With only $N=120$ subjects, $19,900$ edges created an extreme $p \gg n$ regime. The model easily achieved >95% training accuracy, but cross-site generalization collapsed to near 50%, memorizing scanner site hardware noise rather than neural phenotypes.

3. TabPFN Prior vs. CatBoost on Simple Questionnaires

What we tried: Applying the TabPFN tabular foundation model to 5-item and 10-item screening questionnaires.
Why it failed: TabPFN 2.x requires gated HuggingFace authentication and massive memory overhead. On structured 5-to-10 binary trait questions, simple Platt-calibrated CatBoost and Logistic Regression matched or exceeded its performance while executing in <1 ms client-side.

4. Facial / Video Automated Diagnosis

What we rejected: Automated webcam facial landmark or video-based autism classification.
Why it was rejected: Modern clinical and neurodiversity research definitively demonstrates that autistic expressions vary widely across cultures and individuals (masking/camouflaging). Commercial facial analysis tools encode racial and gender biases and lack clinical validity.
